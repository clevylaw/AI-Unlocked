Glossary

Every AI word used in this course, explained simply.

37 terms

AGI (artificial general intelligence)
An AI that could do any thinking task as well as a person. It does not exist yet, and experts disagree about whether or when it will.
AI agent
An AI that takes actions to reach a goal. It might browse the web, fill in forms or write code, as well as reply with text.
AI bias
When an AI gives unfair results for some groups of people. It usually happens because the training data was one-sided.
AI model
The trained AI system inside an app that does the work. ChatGPT is an app. The GPT models are the models inside it.
API
A set of rules that lets one program ask another program for something. Apps send requests to an AI model's API and get replies back.
Artificial intelligence (AI)
Computer systems that do jobs that usually need human thinking, like understanding speech, making choices or writing text.
Chatbot
An app you can talk to in everyday language, such as ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Copilot.
Content credentials
A label attached to a file that records whether and how AI was used to make or change it. Some cameras, apps and websites show it.
Context window
How much text an AI can consider at once, including your conversation and any documents. A bigger window can handle longer material.
Deep learning
A strong kind of machine learning. It uses neural networks with many layers to find complicated patterns.
Deepfake
A realistic fake photo, video or voice recording of a real person, made with AI.
Diffusion model
The kind of AI behind most picture and video tools. It starts with random static and cleans it up into a picture that matches your description.
Embedding
A list of numbers that stands for the meaning of a piece of text. It lets a computer quickly find texts with similar meanings.
Evaluation set
A set of test examples used to measure how well an AI system works. Teams run it again after every change. Also called evals.
Fine-tuning
Extra training for a model that already exists, using special examples. It makes the model better at one task or style.
Generative AI
AI that makes new things, such as text, pictures, sound, video or code. Older AI mostly sorted or checked things that already existed.
Hallucination
When an AI states something false as if it were true. Examples are a made-up source or a wrong date.
Knowledge cutoff
The date a model's training data ends. The model knows nothing after that date unless it searches the web or you give it documents.
Large language model (LLM)
An AI trained on a huge amount of text. It can write, sum up, translate and hold conversations. Chatbots run on these models.
Machine learning
A way of building AI where the computer learns from examples. Nobody writes all its rules by hand. More good examples usually make it better.
MCP (Model Context Protocol)
An open standard for connecting AI apps to tools and information in one standard way.
Multimodal AI
AI that works with several kinds of information, such as text, pictures, sound and video, often at the same time.
Neural network
Layers of simple math, loosely inspired by the brain. Together they turn an input, like a photo, into an output, like the word "cat."
Open model
A model whose trained settings are published, so anyone can download, run and change it. Examples are Meta's Llama and models from Mistral and DeepSeek. Also called an open-weight model.
Parameters
The numbers inside a model that store what it learned. Training adjusts them. Large models have billions of them.
Prompt
The message or instructions you give an AI. Clear, detailed prompts with some background get better results.
Prompt injection
Hidden or harmful instructions inside a document, email or web page. They try to make an AI do something it shouldn't.
Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)
A method where the AI first looks up useful documents and then writes its answer from them. It cuts down on made-up facts.
RLHF (reinforcement learning from human feedback)
Training a model to give the kinds of answers that people rated as better.
System prompt
Instructions given to a model before the conversation starts. They set its role, its tone and its rules.
Temperature
A setting that controls how predictable an AI's answers are. A low setting gives steady answers. A high setting gives more varied and surprising ones.
Thinking model
A model, or a setting in a chatbot, that works through a problem step by step before it answers. It is slower and better at hard tasks. Also called a reasoning model.
Token
A chunk of text, about a word or part of a word. Language models read and write tokens. Usage limits are often counted in tokens.
Tool use
When a model asks an app to run a tool, such as a search or a calculator, and then uses the result in its answer. Also called function calling.
Training
How an AI learns from its training data. It guesses, checks the answer and adjusts itself, millions of times.
Training data
The examples an AI learns from. How good and how varied they are shapes what the AI knows and how fair it is.
Transformer
The design behind almost all modern language models. Google researchers introduced it in 2017. It lets a model compare every word with every other word.
