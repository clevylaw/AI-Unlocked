AI for in-house teams and legal operations

In-house lawyers work for one company. They and their legal operations teams often pick the AI tools the company uses and write the rules for using them.

2 min read · 3 min practice

Two jobs at once

In-house teams use AI for their own work, such as reviewing contracts and answering routine questions from the business. They also advise the whole company on how it uses AI. The second job often matters more.

Using AI in the legal team

  • Contract intake and review for routine agreements, using the company’s playbook.
  • Answering common questions from employees, using the company’s own policies as the source.
  • Tracking cases and the cost of outside law firms, including checking their bills.

Advising the business on AI

  • Approving tools. Review what data a tool touches, where it goes and what the contract promises. The vendor questions resource can help.
  • Spotting high-risk uses. Some AI helps decide who gets hired, who gets a loan or who gets a service. Special laws can apply to those uses. Examples are New York City’s bias-audit law for hiring tools1 and the EU AI Act’s rules for high-risk uses.2
  • Writing the rules. A short company AI policy, training and a simple way for employees to ask before using a new tool.
  • Contracts with customers. Customers increasingly ask how the company uses AI with their data. Contract terms and privacy notices need to match what happens in practice.

Legal operations

Legal operations teams run the business side of a legal department, such as its tools, budget and data. They often lead AI tool trials, measure time saved and train lawyers. The Choosing a legal AI tool lesson covers how to run a fair trial.

For technical readers (optional)

Many companies set up an AI review process that sorts proposed uses by risk. Low-risk uses, such as drafting internal emails, get a quick approval. High-risk uses, such as decisions about hiring, credit or customers’ rights, get a fuller review of data, testing, human oversight and notices. Frameworks such as the NIST AI Risk Management Framework and the EU AI Act’s risk categories are often used as a starting structure.

Try it yourself

Open ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini and paste in this prompt.

I work on a company’s legal team. Draft a one-page intake form that employees fill in before using a new AI tool at work. Include questions about what data it will touch, who will use it and what decisions it will affect. Keep it in plain English.

Check what you learned

The marketing team wants to use a new AI tool to screen job applicants. Why should the legal team review it first?

Answer: B. Using AI in hiring can trigger specific legal duties, such as bias audits and notices to candidates in some places, as well as anti-discrimination law everywhere.
