How chatbots work

ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini run on large language models. A language model writes by guessing the next word, again and again.

2 min read · 3 min practice

Guessing the next word

A language model does one main job. It looks at some text and guesses which word comes next. Then it adds that word and guesses again. In training, it practices this billions of times on a huge amount of writing. After all that practice, it gets good at writing text that reads well.

You can try it below. The bars show how likely the model thinks each next word is.

Why this matters for lawyers

A chatbot writes what sounds likely. A case name that sounds right, with a citation in the right format, is exactly the kind of thing it can produce, whether or not the case exists. The lesson When chatbots get it wrong shows what happened when lawyers forgot this.

What “large” means

A model stores what it learned as billions of numbers that training adjusts. These numbers are called parameters. The biggest models have hundreds of billions of them or more. More parameters usually means more knowledge and skill. It also costs more to run.

What they learn from

Language models learn from books, websites, computer code and other writing, which can include court opinions and statutes that are online. That is why a chatbot can explain a legal idea well. It does not mean it knows the current law where you practice.

How much they understand

Researchers still argue about how much these models understand. For you, one point matters most. Usually the text is helpful, and sometimes it is wrong in ways that are hard to spot. Your own judgment still decides what goes to a client or a court.

For technical readers (optional)

Chat models are a kind of Transformer. Each layer uses a step called self-attention to let every word piece look at the others, then passes the result through a small network. The last layer gives a probability for every possible next piece. A model’s list of pieces, its vocabulary, usually holds about 100,000 to 200,000 of them. The model writes one piece at a time. Legal research tools add a search step before the model writes, which is covered in the lesson Building with AI.
