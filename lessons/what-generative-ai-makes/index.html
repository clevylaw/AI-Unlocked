What generative AI makes

Generative AI can write, sum up, translate, draw and speak. Some of these uses save time in legal work, and some create new risks.

2 min read · 3 min practice

AI that makes things

Older AI mostly checked or sorted things. It answered questions like “Is this email junk?” or “Is this document relevant?” Generative AI makes things. You describe what you want, and it creates something new.

Definition

Generative AI creates new content. It does this using patterns it learned from a huge amount of content made by people.

What it can make

Text
First drafts of letters and emails, summaries of long documents, translations and checklists. Examples are ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and the AI tools inside legal research services.
Speech to text
Written transcripts of recorded meetings and calls, often with a short summary.
Pictures and video
Images and short clips made from a description. Examples are Midjourney, Adobe Firefly, Google Veo and OpenAI Sora.
Voices
Realistic speech in many voices. These tools can also copy a real person’s voice, which matters for fraud and evidence.
Code
Working computer programs from requests in plain English, such as a small script that renames a folder of exhibits.

Why it matters for legal work

A first draft that once took an hour can now take a minute. That saves time on routine writing. The draft still needs a lawyer’s judgment. It may leave out a key fact, use the wrong law or state something false. In legal work, a fast draft is only useful once someone has checked it.

For technical readers (optional)

Different kinds of media have used different designs. Text and code mostly use Transformers. Images, sound and video often use diffusion or a related method called flow matching. More and more, one multimodal model handles every kind of media by turning it all into small pieces called tokens. The output involves some chance, so the same request can give different results. Settings such as temperature and the random seed control how much the results vary. For legal drafting, that variation is a reason to review every version you get.

Try it yourself

Open ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini and paste in this prompt.

Use this made-up fact pattern. A tenant in a city apartment has had no heat for three weeks in January. The landlord has not answered two emails. Write a short, polite letter from the tenant asking for repairs. Then rewrite it as a plain-language summary a 12-year-old could understand.
