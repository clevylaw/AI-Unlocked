AI at work

AI is changing everyday work. This lesson shows how to use it at your job without breaking the rules.

Useful in almost any job

  • Getting ready for meetings. Paste in the agenda and ask what questions each person might raise.
  • Summaries. Turn a long report into five main points.
  • Spreadsheets. Describe what you need and ask for the formula.
  • Presentations. Describe your message and your audience, and ask for an outline.
  • Looking for a job. Ask for help fitting your résumé and cover letter to a specific job ad.

Important

Many employers have rules about which AI tools you may use and what you may paste into them. Never put private client details, personal information or company secrets into a public chatbot until you have checked your employer's rules.

AI inside the apps you use at work

Microsoft 365, Google Workspace, Zoom, Slack and Notion now include AI helpers. They can sum up meetings, draft documents and search your files. If your employer gives you these tools, they are usually the safest choice for work information.

Staying valuable at work

People who learn to use AI well can work faster and take on more. Some skills are hard for AI to copy. Good judgment, strong relationships, deep knowledge of your field and good taste become even more valuable.

For technical readers (optional)

Business plans for AI tools usually add extra protections. These include a promise not to train on your data, keeping your data for a short time or not at all, signing in with your work account, records of who did what, and controls for administrators. If you build tools for your own company, connect the AI to company files in a way that respects each person's access rights. One way to do this is with MCP servers, which are connectors that give an AI controlled access to other systems. That way, the AI can only see what the person using it is allowed to see. Keep a record of prompts and answers so they can be reviewed.

Try it yourself

Open ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Copilot and paste in this prompt.

In a spreadsheet, column A has dates and column B has sales amounts. Write a formula that adds up sales for March 2026 only. Explain each part of the formula in plain English.
