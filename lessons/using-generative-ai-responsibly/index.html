Using generative AI responsibly

Before you make and share things with AI, there are a few risks and rules to know about.

Who owns what AI makes

Courts and governments are still deciding this. In several countries, work made entirely by AI cannot be protected by copyright. There are also court cases about how AI companies used other people's work to train their AI. If you want to use AI-made work for a business, read the tool's terms of service first.

Deepfakes

A deepfake is a fake photo, voice or video of a real person, made with AI. Some uses are good, like special effects in films. AI has also given a voice back to people who lost the ability to speak. Deepfakes are also used for scams and to spread lies.

Before you share a picture or video

AI pictures are now so realistic that checking for odd-looking hands no longer works well. Instead, find out where the picture came from. Look for a label such as "Made with AI." Then look for the same story on a news site you trust.

Scam calls with copied voices

AI can copy someone's voice from a few seconds of recording. Agree on a secret family password. Ask for it if someone calls and urgently asks for money.

Energy and water

Training and running large AI systems uses a lot of electricity. The buildings full of computers that run AI, called data centers, also use water to stay cool. You can do your small part by making only the versions you need.

Say when you used AI

If AI helped you make something for work or school, say so. People trust you more when you are open about it. Many schools and employers now have rules about using AI, so check yours.

For technical readers (optional)

A standard called C2PA attaches signed "content credentials" to a file. These record how the file was made and changed. Some companies also hide invisible watermarks in AI-made media. Google's SynthID is one example. Tools that claim to detect AI-written text or AI pictures are unreliable. They often flag human work as AI-made, so never treat their answer as proof. The legal arguments are mainly about permission to use training data, who owns what AI makes, and the right to control your own face and voice. The rules differ from country to country.

Try it yourself

Open ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini and paste in this prompt.

I got a voice message from a relative asking me to send money right away. Give me a short checklist to tell whether it might be a scam using an AI copy of their voice.
