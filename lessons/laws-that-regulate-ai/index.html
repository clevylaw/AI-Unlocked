Laws that regulate AI

Lawmakers are writing rules for AI itself. Clients will ask about them, and they change often.

The European Union's AI Act

The EU AI Act is the most complete AI law so far. It sorts AI systems by risk.

Banned
A short list of uses seen as too harmful, such as social scoring that leads to unfair treatment of people.
High risk
Uses that affect people's lives, such as hiring, credit, education and access to public services. These carry strict duties for testing, records, human oversight and transparency.
Lower risk
Lighter duties, such as telling people they are talking to a chatbot or that content was made by AI.

The law applies in stages. In June 2026, the EU gave final approval to changes that moved the main high-risk deadlines to December 2, 2027, and August 2, 2028. The law can also apply to companies outside the EU that offer AI in the EU. Always check the current dates.

The United States

The U.S. has no single law that covers all AI. Rules come from several places.

  • Existing laws. Laws against discrimination, unfair business practices and privacy violations already apply when AI is used.
  • States. Colorado passed an AI law in 2024 about high-risk AI in big decisions about people. A 2025 law pushed its start date back to June 30, 2026. Check the Colorado legislature's website for any later changes. Other states have passed narrower laws.
  • Cities. New York City's Local Law 144 requires bias audits for AI hiring tools.
  • Courts and bars. Court orders and ethics opinions govern how lawyers use AI, as earlier lessons showed.

Important

AI law is changing quickly. Treat anything in this lesson as a starting point, and check current official sources before advising a client.

For technical readers (optional)

The EU AI Act entered into force in August 2024 and applies in stages. Bans on certain practices came first, then rules for general-purpose AI models, then rules for high-risk systems. In June 2026, the Council of the EU gave final approval to a package of changes called the digital omnibus on AI. It moves the high-risk deadlines to December 2, 2027, for stand-alone systems and August 2, 2028, for AI built into regulated products. Check the version in force on EUR-Lex before advising anyone. In April 2023, several U.S. federal agencies, including the FTC and the EEOC, issued a joint statement that existing laws on consumer protection and discrimination apply to AI. Agency priorities have shifted since, so check current guidance.
