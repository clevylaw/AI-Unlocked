AI and legal jobs

Many people in law wonder what AI means for their jobs. This lesson looks at which tasks are changing and how to get ready.

2 min read · 3 min practice

Tasks AI changes most and least

AI is changing tasks that repeat the same steps across many documents. These include first-pass document review, summarizing depositions and contracts, routine drafting and basic research.

It is changing much less the tasks that depend on judgment, trust and people. These include advising clients, negotiating, arguing in court, managing a case strategy and handling sensitive conversations.

An honest concern

Junior lawyers and paralegals have long learned by doing routine work, such as reviewing documents. If AI does more of that work, firms will need new ways to train people. Expect this to change how new lawyers learn the job.

Jobs that are growing

Legal teams now need people who can choose AI tools, test them, write policies and train others. Titles include legal technologist, legal operations specialist and knowledge management lawyer. Courts and regulators also need lawyers who understand AI.

How to get ready

  • Use approved AI tools for parts of your work, and learn where they fail.
  • Get skilled at checking AI output, including citations, facts and dates.
  • Build skills AI finds hard, such as judgment, writing for a specific reader, negotiation and client care.
  • Learn the rules on AI in your jurisdiction. The module Your professional duties covers them.
For technical readers (optional)

Studies of AI help in real workplaces, such as customer support, writing and coding, have often found the biggest gains for less experienced workers. Economists separate tasks from jobs. AI usually takes over or helps with specific tasks, so most jobs change shape before they disappear. For law, a useful exercise is to list your tasks and mark which are routine, which need judgment and which need a human relationship.

Try it yourself

Open ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini and paste in this prompt.

My job is [describe your legal job or the job you want, in two sentences]. Which of my tasks could AI help with today? Which tasks need human judgment and are unlikely to change? What is one skill I should build next?
