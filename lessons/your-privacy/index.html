Your privacy

What you type into an AI tool may be saved. This lesson shows what can happen to it and how to protect yourself.

Your chats may be saved and used

What you type into a chatbot may be saved. Depending on the app and your settings, staff may read it to check for safety problems. It may also be used to train future AI. That can include health worries, money problems or other personal details.

What to do

Open the privacy settings in each AI app you use. Most apps let you stop your chats from being used for training. Many also let you start a temporary chat that is not saved, and delete your history.

Never share these

  • Passwords, PINs or security codes.
  • Bank, card or government ID numbers.
  • Other people's private information, unless they said yes.
  • Your employer's private information.

Work and school accounts

Paid plans for businesses and schools usually protect your data better. For example, they often promise not to train AI on it. If your workplace gives you one of these accounts, use it for work tasks.

For technical readers (optional)

Check whether a company offers zero data retention, which means it does not keep what you send. Also check whether it can keep your data in a chosen country, and whether the contract promises not to train on your data. For data that must stay private, you have a few options. You can remove personal details before sending anything. You can run an open model on your own computers. Or you can use a private setup from a cloud company. Remember that prompts may also be saved in your own app's logs.

Try it yourself

Open The AI app you use most and paste in this prompt.

Open the app's settings. Find out whether your chats are used for training, how to start a temporary chat, and how to delete your chat history. If you can't find these settings, ask the chatbot: "Where are your privacy settings, and what does each one do?"

The AI tools page links to each app. Most have a free plan.
