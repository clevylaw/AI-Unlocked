Your duties when you use AI

No new rule book is needed. The ethics rules lawyers already follow apply to AI, and bars have explained how.

2 min read · 3 min practice

The main guidance

In July 2024, the American Bar Association issued Formal Opinion 512 on generative AI.1 It does not create new rules. It explains how the existing rules of professional conduct apply when lawyers use AI. Many state bars have issued their own guidance too. Florida’s Opinion 24-1 came out in January 2024.2 California published practical guidance in 2023 and updated it in 2026.3

Check your own rules

The ABA’s opinions are guidance, not binding law. Your state’s rules and ethics opinions are the ones that apply to you, and they can differ. This course is general education, not legal advice.

The six duties in plain language

Competence
Understand what the tool does well and badly, enough to use it safely. Keep learning as it changes. Check its work.
Confidentiality
Protect client information. Know what the tool does with what you type. Get the client’s informed consent when the rules require it.
Communication
Tell clients about AI use when it matters to them, such as when they ask, when it affects the fee or when their information goes into a tool.
Candor
Never give a court false statements of law or fact. Everything you file must be checked, whoever or whatever wrote the first draft.
Supervision
Make sure associates, paralegals, staff and vendors use AI properly. Set policies and train people.
Fees
Charge reasonable fees. Bill only for time actually spent, and explain any charges for AI tools.

For law students

These duties start when you practice, but habits start now. Follow your school’s honor code. Practice checking AI output as if a client depended on it. Many legal employers will ask how you use AI.

For technical readers (optional)

Opinion 512 ties each duty to the ABA Model Rules: competence to Rule 1.1, confidentiality to Rules 1.6, 1.9(c) and 1.18(b), communication to Rule 1.4, candor and meritorious claims to Rules 3.1, 3.3 and 8.4(c), supervision to Rules 5.1 and 5.3, and fees to Rule 1.5. The Model Rules are a template. Each state adopts its own version, sometimes with changes, and state ethics opinions can differ from the ABA’s.

Check what you learned

According to ABA Formal Opinion 512, what level of understanding of AI do lawyers need?

Answer: C. The opinion says lawyers need a reasonable understanding of the benefits and risks of the tools they use. They do not need to be experts, but they must keep learning as the tools change.
