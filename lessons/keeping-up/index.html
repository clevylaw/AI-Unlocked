Keeping up

AI changes fast. You can stay informed without feeling swamped.

How fast things change

A few years ago, AI could barely write a paragraph. Now it can write software, hold spoken conversations and make realistic video. New models come out every few months. What seems amazing today will seem normal next year.

A simple routine

  • Pick one or two sources and check them once a week. Examples are MIT Technology Review, the AI section of The Verge, or a newsletter you trust.
  • Use the tools. Ten minutes trying a new feature teaches you more than an hour of reading about it.
  • Come back to this site. The AI tools and AI models pages are updated every week.

A healthy way to think about it

Nobody knows everything about AI. You understand the main ideas from this course. If you keep practicing with the tools that matter to you, you will keep up.

You have finished the main course

You now know what AI is and how chatbots and other generative AI work. You know how to choose a model and how to use AI for writing, learning and work. You also know the real risks and where AI is heading. Keep trying new things, and stay curious.

For technical readers (optional)

For more technical detail, read the model cards and system cards that companies publish with each new model. Be careful with ranking tables of test scores. Skim new research papers on arXiv in the cs.CL and cs.LG sections. Companies' change logs and API guides are where new features appear first. Building a small project with each new model teaches you more than any summary.

Try it yourself

Open ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini and paste in this prompt.

I'm interested in [your interests]. Suggest one AI tool I haven't tried yet, and a 15-minute first project I could do with it this week.

The AI tools page links to each app. Most have a free plan.
