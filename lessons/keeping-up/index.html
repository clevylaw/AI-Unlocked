Keeping up

AI and the rules about it change fast. You can stay informed without reading everything.

2 min read · 3 min practice

How fast things change

A few years ago, AI could barely write a paragraph. Now it drafts documents, holds spoken conversations and reviews contracts. New models come out every few months. Courts, bars and lawmakers are writing new rules about AI at the same time.

A simple routine

  • Know the rules that apply to you. Check your bar’s ethics guidance, the rules of the courts you appear in and your employer’s or school’s AI policy.
  • Follow one or two trusted sources. Examples are the ABA Journal, your state bar’s news and a legal technology newsletter you trust.
  • Use the tools. Ten minutes trying a feature on a made-up problem teaches more than an hour of reading.
  • Come back to this site. The AI tools and AI models pages are updated every week.

Where to go next

If you are a lawyer, paralegal or law student, the module Your professional duties covers competence, confidentiality, courts and checking AI research. The module Legal AI tools and AI law covers choosing tools and the laws that regulate AI.

For technical readers (optional)

For more technical detail, read the model cards and system cards that companies publish with each new model. For legal tools, ask vendors for their own test results and how they measured accuracy. Academic studies such as the Stanford RegLab study of legal research tools are a good check on vendor claims. Court standing orders and local rules are usually posted on each court’s website.

Try it yourself

Open ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini and paste in this prompt.

I work in [your area of law or your year of law school]. Suggest one AI task I could practice this week using only made-up or public information, and the steps to check the result.

The AI tools page links to each app. Most have a free plan.

Check what you learned

What is the most reliable way to keep up with the rules on AI in legal work?

Answer: C. Ethics opinions, court rules and your employer’s policy are the rules that apply to you. One or two trusted news sources tell you when they change.
