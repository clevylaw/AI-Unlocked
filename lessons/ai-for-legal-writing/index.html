AI for legal writing

AI can help you write faster and more clearly. The legal judgment, and the responsibility, stay with you.

2 min read · 3 min practice

Where AI helps

The hardest part of writing is often getting started. AI is good at writing a first draft that you can then fix. It is also good at shortening, reordering and changing the tone of text you already wrote.

  • Plain-language versions. Turn a dense paragraph into something a client can follow.
  • First drafts of routine letters. Engagement letter cover notes, scheduling emails and status updates.
  • Editing. Ask it to “cut this by a third,” “fix the grammar” or “make the headings parallel.”
  • Checklists and outlines. Turn your notes into an outline for a memo or a list of documents to request.

A good writing prompt

“Write a short email to a client explaining that the hearing has moved from March 3 to March 17 and that they do not need to do anything yet. Use a warm, plain tone and no legal jargon. Use the name Jordan.” Then you replace the made-up name and check every date before sending.

Where it can hurt you

AI drafts can state the law wrongly, leave out a key fact or invent a quote. They can also sound confident about things you never said. For anything that goes to a client, an opposing party or a court, you must check every fact, date and legal point.

Keep your own voice

AI writing often sounds bland, like it could be about any case. Clients and judges notice. Rewrite the draft in your own words. Add the facts and reasons that only you know. Cut anything you would not say yourself.

For technical readers (optional)

To get the same style across many documents, put your style guide and a few approved examples in the system prompt. Ask for a fixed structure, such as headings for facts, issue, rule and next steps. A lower temperature setting makes the wording more consistent from one try to the next. Some firms keep a library of approved clauses and have the system look up and reuse them, which is usually more reliable than retraining a model, called fine-tuning.

Try it yourself

Open ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini and paste in this prompt.

Rewrite this paragraph so a client with no legal training can understand it, in under 100 words: “The court’s denial of defendant’s motion for summary judgment means the matter will proceed to trial, absent settlement, as genuine disputes of material fact remain regarding the parties’ respective obligations under the agreement.”
