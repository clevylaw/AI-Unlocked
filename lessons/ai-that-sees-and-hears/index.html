AI that sees and hears

Today’s AI can read scanned pages, photos and handwriting, and it can turn speech into text. That helps with evidence and with recording rules.

2 min read · 3 min practice

Multimodal AI

Multimodal means working with several kinds of information at once, such as text, pictures and sound. Most of the leading AI models are now multimodal.

Reading documents

AI can read scanned pages, photos of documents and some handwriting. It can turn a box of scanned records into searchable text or pull dates and amounts into a table. It can misread numbers and names, so check anything important against the original page.

Turning speech into text

AI can transcribe meetings, interviews and voicemails and then sum them up. Before you record anyone, check the law. In some U.S. states, everyone on a call must agree to it being recorded. Recording tools also receive confidential information, so they must be approved for that use.

Not a court reporter

An AI transcript is a working draft. It can mishear legal terms, names and numbers. Do not treat it as an official record of a deposition or hearing.

Talking out loud

Voice modes in ChatGPT, Gemini and other apps let you talk with the AI out loud and get an answer in about a second. That can help you rehearse an opening statement or practice explaining a point to a client. Keep confidential details out of these conversations unless the tool is approved for them.

For technical readers (optional)

Pictures are cut into small squares, and each square becomes a token, so pictures and text can go through the same model. Speech-to-text models turn sound into text and can label who is speaking, though they make more mistakes with accents, cross-talk and legal terms. Reading text from scans is called optical character recognition, or OCR. Modern AI does it better than older tools, but it can still misread numbers, which matters in contracts and financial records.

Try it yourself

Open The ChatGPT, Gemini or Claude app on your phone and paste in this prompt.

Take a photo of a printed page that has nothing private on it, such as a public notice or a page from a library book. Ask: “Type out this text exactly, then list anything you could not read clearly.” Compare its version with the page.
