AI that sees and hears

Today's AI can work with photos, voices and video as well as text.

Multimodal AI

Multimodal means working with several kinds of information at once. That includes text, pictures, sound and video. Most of the leading AI models are now multimodal.

Try these with your phone's camera

Take a photo of a math problem and ask for an explanation, step by step. Take a photo of the inside of your fridge and ask what you could cook. Take a photo of a confusing form or bill and ask what it means.

Talking out loud

Voice modes in ChatGPT, Gemini and other apps let you talk with the AI out loud. It answers in about a second, and you can interrupt it, as you would a person. Some can also see through your phone's camera while you talk.

AI in science

Some of AI's biggest effects are in research. Proteins are tiny parts of every living thing, and their shape decides what they do. DeepMind's AlphaFold predicted the shapes of hundreds of millions of proteins. That work earned a share of the 2024 Nobel Prize in Chemistry. AI is now speeding up the search for new medicines and new materials. It is also helping with weather forecasts.

For technical readers (optional)

Pictures are cut into small squares, and each square becomes a token. That lets pictures and text go through the same Transformer. Newer voice models work on sound tokens directly. Older systems chained three steps together: turning speech into text, answering in text, and turning the answer back into speech. Working on sound directly is faster and keeps the tone of voice. AlphaFold 2 combines attention-based networks with information about how proteins evolved and how their parts fit in space.

Try it yourself

Open The ChatGPT, Gemini or Claude app on your phone and paste in this prompt.

Take a photo of something near you, such as a plant, a label or a gadget. Then ask: "What is this, and what is one interesting fact about it?"

The AI tools page links to each app. Most have a free plan.
