Asking good questions

The way you ask changes the answer you get. Small changes to your question can give you a much better reply.

Your words matter

The message you type is called a prompt. A vague prompt gets a vague answer. A specific prompt gets a specific, useful answer.

Vague prompt

"Tell me about diets."

Usual answer

A general list of a dozen kinds of diets. None of it fits you in particular.

Specific prompt

"I'm 45 and want to lose 5 kilograms in 3 months. I don't want to stop eating bread. Suggest a simple eating plan and explain why it works."

Usual answer

A clear weekly plan that fits your goal and your limits, with reasons for each part.

What to put in a good prompt

  • Who you are. Say what you already know about the topic.
  • What you want. Describe the task in as much detail as you can.
  • Your limits. Mention length, tone, budget or anything to avoid.
  • The shape of the answer. Ask for a list, a table, an email or a few options to choose from.

Keep the conversation going

Your first prompt does not need to be perfect. Reply with things like "make it shorter," "make it more casual," "give me other options" or "explain step 2." Chatbots remember the conversation, so improving the answer bit by bit is normal.

What chatbots do well and less well

Chatbots are good at explaining things simply, writing drafts and coming up with ideas. They can also sum up long documents, rewrite text in a different tone, translate and write computer code.

They are weaker with very recent news, unless the chatbot can search the web. They also make mistakes with exact numbers and sources. And they often do not notice when they don't know something.

For technical readers (optional)

Behind the chat window, your message is joined to the earlier conversation and to hidden instructions from the company, called the system prompt. The model reads all of it as one long string of word pieces. Some methods make a measurable difference. They include giving a clear role and task, showing a few examples of good answers (called few-shot prompting), asking the model to work step by step on hard problems, saying exactly what format you want, and breaking a big job into a chain of smaller prompts. Very long prompts cost more and can spread the model's attention thin. Aim for specific, which is different from long.

Try it yourself

Open ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini and paste in this prompt.

I'm planning a 3-day trip to Lisbon in spring with two kids aged 8 and 11. We like food and history but not long museum visits. Suggest a plan for each day in a table, with morning, afternoon and evening.

The AI tools page links to each app. Most have a free plan.
