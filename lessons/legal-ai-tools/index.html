Legal AI tools

Many AI tools are built for legal work. They fall into a few groups, and each has its own strengths and risks.

2 min read · 3 min practice

The main kinds of legal AI tools

Research
AI features inside legal research services, such as Lexis+ with Protégé (called Lexis+ AI until February 2026) and Westlaw’s AI tools. You ask a question in plain English and get an answer with links to cases and statutes.
Drafting and contracts
Tools that draft clauses, compare contract versions or flag risky terms. Some work inside Microsoft Word.
Document review
E-discovery platforms that rank and sort documents in a lawsuit, and newer tools that sum up and answer questions about large sets of documents.
Legal assistants
General-purpose AI platforms built for law firms and legal departments, which combine research, drafting and document review.
Practice management
AI features in the software firms use for time tracking, billing, intake and client communication.

The AI tools page lists some well-known tools in each group, with links. It is a starting point, not a recommendation.

What they do better than general chatbots

Legal tools are usually connected to real, current legal sources. They often come with contracts that protect client data, and they are built around legal tasks. For client work, this makes them a better fit than a free consumer chatbot.

What they still get wrong

They can still give wrong answers. The Stanford study found that leading legal research tools gave false or misleading answers between 17 and 33 percent of the time,1 including answers that cited real cases for points those cases do not support. A link to a real case is a good start, not proof. Everything in the lesson Checking AI legal research still applies.

For technical readers (optional)

Most legal AI products combine a general language model, licensed from a large AI company or run privately, with the vendor’s own data and workflows. Examples are a database of cases, a library of clauses, or a document review platform. The vendor’s value is often in the data, the checking steps and the security, as much as in the model. That is why it is worth asking which model a product uses and what the vendor adds.

Try it yourself

Open ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini and paste in this prompt.

I work in [your type of legal work, such as a small family law practice or a corporate legal department]. Which kinds of legal AI tools might help with my work, and what is one risk to check for each kind? Don’t recommend specific products. Describe the kinds of tools instead.

The AI tools page links to each app. Most have a free plan.

Check what you learned

How do legal research AI tools usually differ from general chatbots?

Answer: B. Legal research tools ground their answers in a database of real legal sources and link to them. That makes them much more useful for research, though they still make mistakes.
