Choosing an AI model

ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini each offer several models. This lesson explains how they differ and how to pick one.

The app and the model

The app is what you use, such as ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Copilot. The model is the AI system inside the app that does the work. Most apps let you switch between several models. Companies release new models every few months, which is why the names keep changing.

Large, medium and small models

Most companies offer a family of models in different sizes.

Large
The smartest and slowest. Best for hard problems, long documents and careful writing. Free plans often limit how much you can use them.
Medium
The everyday choice. Fast, and good enough for most tasks.
Small
Very fast and cheap to run. Good for quick questions, short rewrites and apps that need speed.

The names give hints about size. Anthropic's Claude models go from Haiku (small) to Sonnet (medium) to Opus (large). Words like "mini," "flash" or "lite" in a name usually mean a smaller, faster model.

Thinking models

Some models take extra time to work through a problem step by step before they answer. Some apps call this a "thinking" or "reasoning" setting. These models are slower. They are much better at math, planning, tricky logic and computer code. Use them when getting the answer right matters more than speed.

Other ways models differ

  • How much they can read at once. This is called the context window. A bigger one is better for long reports or books.
  • Pictures and sound. Most current models can read photos, PDF files and screenshots. Many can talk out loud.
  • Web search. Some can look up current information online.
  • Open models. Some models, such as Meta's Llama, can be downloaded and run on your own computer. Your information then stays on your computer.

Which one to use

If you want toTry
Ask everyday questions or write an emailThe standard model in any of the big chatbots
Solve a hard problem or check your thinkingA thinking model
Get current facts with sourcesA chatbot that can search the web, or Perplexity
Sum up a long PDF or reportA large model with a big context window
Keep private information on your own computerAn open model that runs on your computer

Good to know

The best models from the big companies are close in quality. Pick one and learn it well. Try another one when a task is not going well. The AI models page shows what is new.

For technical readers (optional)

Compare models on a set of your own test questions. Public rankings help less, because many models now score near the top and some may have seen the test questions in training. Useful things to compare are quality on your task, speed (how long until the first word appears, and how many word pieces per second), price per million word pieces in and out, how much text it can read at once, usage limits, and how the company handles your data. Two features can cut costs a lot. Prompt caching saves money when you send the same long text again and again. Batch processing is cheaper for work that does not need an answer right away.

Try it yourself

Open Any two of ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini and paste in this prompt.

A bat and a ball cost $1.10 in total. The bat costs $1.00 more than the ball. How much does the ball cost? Explain how you worked it out. (Ask two different models, or one model with thinking turned on and then off, and compare the answers.)
