About this site

What AI for Legal Work is, how it stays up to date, and what happens to your progress.

What this site is

AI for Legal Work is a free course about artificial intelligence for legal work. It is for law students, lawyers, paralegals, legal staff and legal technologists. It has 30 short lessons in 9 modules, with real legal examples such as court sanctions for fake AI citations and the American Bar Association’s ethics opinion on AI. Each lesson has a prompt to try in a real AI tool and one question to check what you learned. There is no account, no fee and no advertising.

You can follow one of 4 learning paths, or read the lessons in any order. A short placement check lets you skip lessons you already know. When you finish a path, or the whole course, you can save a certificate.

How it stays up to date

Every week, the lists on the AI tools and AI models pages are checked for new releases and changes. Each change has to be confirmed by the company’s own announcement or by two trusted news sites. The “last checked” date on those pages shows when the last check happened.

The weekly check also flags lessons that may be out of date, so they can be fixed.

Your progress stays in your browser

Your progress, your learning path, your certificates and your read-aloud settings are saved in this browser, on this device. They are not sent to anyone. If you clear your browser data, or use another device or browser, your progress will not be there.

To clear your progress yourself, use Start over at the bottom of any page.

The fonts on this site come from Google Fonts, so your browser asks Google’s servers for them. Links to AI tools take you to other companies’ websites, which have their own privacy rules.

Listening to lessons

Every lesson has a Listen button. The recorded voice (Harper) was made with Speechify, a text-to-speech service. You can also choose one of the voices built into your device. Voices with names like Natural, Neural, Premium, Enhanced or Siri usually sound the most human.

Not legal advice

The lessons are general education. They are not legal advice, and they do not create a lawyer-client relationship. Most examples come from the United States. Ethics rules, court rules and AI laws differ by country, state and court, and they change often. Check the current rules that apply to you.

Completing the course does not earn continuing legal education (CLE) credit.

Corrections

If you spot a mistake or an out-of-date rule, use Report a correction at the end of any lesson or at the bottom of any page. It opens a short form on GitHub, which needs a free GitHub account. Please include a source if you have one.

Sources

Lessons that describe court cases, ethics opinions, laws or studies end with a list of sources. Some sources need a subscription. Check the original documents before relying on them.
