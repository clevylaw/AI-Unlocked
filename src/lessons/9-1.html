The main kinds of legal AI tools

Research
AI features inside legal research services, such as Lexis+ with Protégé (called Lexis+ AI until February 2026) and Westlaw's AI tools. You ask a question in plain English and get an answer with links to cases and statutes.
Drafting and contracts
Tools that draft clauses, compare contract versions or flag risky terms. Some work inside Microsoft Word.
Document review
E-discovery tools that rank and sort the documents in a lawsuit. Newer tools can also sum up large sets of documents and answer questions about them.
Legal assistants
AI tools built for law firms and legal departments that do many jobs in one place, such as research, drafting and document review.
Practice management
AI features in the software firms use to track time, send bills, take in new clients and stay in touch with them.

The AI tools page lists some well-known tools in each group, with links. It is a starting point, not a recommendation.

What they do better than general chatbots

Legal tools are usually connected to real, current legal sources. They often come with contracts that protect client data, and they are built around legal tasks. For client work, this makes them a better fit than a free consumer chatbot.

What they still get wrong

They can still give wrong answers. The Stanford study found that leading legal research tools gave false or misleading answers between 17 and 33 percent of the time.[[1]] Some answers cited real cases for points those cases do not make. A link to a real case is a good start, not proof. Everything in the lesson Checking AI legal research still applies.
