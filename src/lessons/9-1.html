The main kinds of legal AI tools

Research
AI features inside legal research services, such as Lexis+ AI and Westlaw's AI tools. You ask a question in plain English and get an answer with links to cases and statutes.
Drafting and contracts
Tools that draft clauses, compare contract versions or flag risky terms. Some work inside Microsoft Word.
Document review
E-discovery platforms that rank and sort documents in a lawsuit, and newer tools that sum up and answer questions about large sets of documents.
Legal assistants
General-purpose AI platforms built for law firms and legal departments, which combine research, drafting and document review.
Practice management
AI features in the software firms use for time tracking, billing, intake and client communication.

The AI tools page lists some well-known tools in each group, with links. It is a starting point, not a recommendation.

What they do better than general chatbots

Legal tools are usually connected to real, current legal sources. They often come with contracts that protect client data, and they are built around legal tasks. For client work, this makes them a better fit than a free consumer chatbot.

What they still get wrong

They can still give wrong answers. The Stanford study found that leading legal research tools gave false or misleading answers between 17 and 33 percent of the time, including answers that cited real cases for points those cases do not support. A link to a real case is a good start, not proof. Everything in the lesson Checking AI legal research still applies.
