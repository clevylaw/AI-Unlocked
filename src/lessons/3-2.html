Your words matter

The message you type is called a prompt. A vague prompt gets a vague answer. A specific prompt gets a specific, useful answer.

Vague prompt

"Tell me about landlord-tenant law."

Usual answer

A general overview that fits no state in particular and no real problem.

Specific prompt

"I'm a paralegal in Illinois. Make a checklist of the facts to collect from a tenant who says the landlord kept the security deposit. Put it in a table, and list the statute I should read to confirm the rules."

Usual answer

A practical checklist you can use, plus a pointer to the law you must check yourself.

What to put in a good prompt

  • Who you are. Say your role, such as student, paralegal or associate, and what you already know.
  • Where. Name the country, state or court. Law differs from place to place.
  • The task. Say exactly what you want, such as a checklist, an outline or a plain-language summary.
  • The shape of the answer. Ask for a table, bullet points or a set length.
  • Honesty. Ask it to say what it is unsure about and which sources you should check.

Leave client details out

Describe the problem with made-up names and only the facts the AI needs. Do not paste in client names, private documents or anything covered by your duty of confidentiality, unless your employer has approved the tool for that use. The lesson Confidentiality and privilege explains why.

Keep the conversation going

Your first prompt does not need to be perfect. Reply with things like "make it shorter," "write it for a client with no legal training" or "what facts would change your answer?" Chatbots remember the conversation, so improving the answer bit by bit is normal.

What chatbots do well and less well

Chatbots are good at explaining ideas, making outlines and checklists, and rewriting text for a different reader. They are also good at summing up a long document you give them.

They are weak at exact legal sources. They can invent cases and quotes, mix up states and miss recent changes in the law.
