Where AI helps in transactional work

  • First-pass review. Flag clauses such as change of control, assignment, exclusivity, auto-renewal and limits on liability across many contracts.
  • Playbook checks. Compare a counterparty's draft against your organization's preferred and fallback positions.
  • Comparing versions. Explain in plain English what changed between two drafts.
  • Due diligence. Sum up a data room and build a table of key terms for a deal team.
  • Plain-language summaries of key terms for a business client.

The risks that matter most

  • Missed terms. Unusual wording, side letters and amendments are easy to miss. Check a sample by hand, including documents the tool cleared.
  • Wrong law. Whether a clause is enforceable depends on the governing law and the facts. AI often answers as if one rule applies everywhere.
  • Confidentiality. Deal documents are often covered by confidentiality agreements that limit who may see them. Check those terms and use only approved tools.
  • Numbers. Dates, amounts and notice periods can be misread, especially from scans. Check them against the original.

A good working habit

Ask the tool to quote the exact clause and say where it appears, such as the section number. A quote you can find in seconds is far easier to check than a paraphrase.

Your judgment still decides

AI can tell you a clause exists. It cannot tell you whether the client should accept it. That depends on the deal, the client's goals and the negotiating position, which is the lawyer's job.
