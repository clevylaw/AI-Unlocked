Tasks AI changes most and least

AI is changing tasks that repeat the same steps across many documents. Examples are a first look through documents in a lawsuit, summing up testimony and contracts, routine drafting and basic research.

It is changing much less the tasks that depend on judgment, trust and people. Examples are advising clients, negotiating, arguing in court, planning how to handle a case and having hard talks with people.

Training new lawyers

Junior lawyers and paralegals have long learned by doing routine work, such as reviewing documents. If AI does more of that work, firms will need new ways to train people. Expect this to change how new lawyers learn the job.

Jobs that are growing

Legal teams now need people who can choose AI tools, test them, write policies and train others. Job titles include legal technologist, legal operations specialist and knowledge management lawyer, a lawyer who keeps a firm's know-how organized. Courts and regulators also need lawyers who understand AI.

How to get ready

  • Use approved AI tools for parts of your work, and learn where they fail.
  • Get skilled at checking AI output, including citations, facts and dates.
  • Build skills AI finds hard, such as judgment, writing for a specific reader, negotiation and client care.
  • Learn the rules on AI where you practice or study. The module Your professional duties covers them.
