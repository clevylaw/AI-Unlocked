Learning from examples

A normal computer program follows rules that a person writes. For example, "If the room is warmer than 30 degrees, turn on the fan." Someone has to think of every rule ahead of time.

AI works another way. You give it thousands of examples instead of rules. You might show it 10,000 photos of cats and 10,000 photos of other things. The AI then works out for itself what makes a cat look like a cat.

Think of it this way

Nobody gave you a rule book for reading handwriting. You saw the letter A written in many different ways, and your brain learned what an A looks like. AI learns in a similar way.

Training data

The examples an AI learns from are called training data. Their quality matters a lot. An AI that learned mostly from English text will be better at English than at other languages. An AI that learned from photos of people in one part of the world may be worse at recognizing faces from other places.

This is how AI can become biased, which means unfair to some groups of people. If the examples are unfair or one-sided, the AI learns that too. Data made by people often is one-sided.

Practice, millions of times

During training, the AI makes a guess and checks whether it was right. Then it changes itself a tiny bit and tries again. It does this millions of times. Each small fix makes the next guess a little better. Nobody writes the rules. They grow out of all that practice.

Example

The AI in a self-driving car studies millions of hours of driving videos. It learns to slow down when a person steps off the curb. No programmer wrote that rule. The AI picked it up from the examples.
