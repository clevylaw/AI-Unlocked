Where bias comes from

AI learns from data made by people. That data reflects our history, including the unfair parts. If an AI learns from one-sided examples, it picks up the same slant. Then it repeats that slant for many people at once.

Real examples

One hiring tool learned from a company's past hiring. It favored men for tech jobs, because the company had hired more men before. Some face recognition tools made more mistakes with people who have darker skin. They had learned mostly from photos of people with lighter skin.

Why it matters

AI now helps decide who gets a job interview, a loan or medical care. If the AI is biased, real people get hurt. It can also be harder to argue with than a person, because you can't always see how it made its decision.

What people are doing about it

Researchers test AI for unfair results and look for ways to fix them. New laws, like the European Union's AI Act, require extra checks when AI is used for big decisions about people. If AI played a part in an important decision about you, you can often ask how the decision was made.
