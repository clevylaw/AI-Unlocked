The app and the model

The app is what you use, such as ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or a legal research service. The model is the AI system inside the app that does the work. Most apps let you switch between several models. Companies release new ones every few months, which is why the names keep changing.

General chatbots and legal tools

General chatbots
ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Copilot. Good for explaining ideas, outlining, rewriting and summing up documents you give them. Unreliable for finding legal authority on their own.
Legal research tools
AI features inside services such as Lexis+ AI and Westlaw, and other legal AI products. They search real legal databases before answering and link to sources. They still make mistakes.
Practice tools
Tools for contract review, e-discovery and drafting. They are built for one kind of legal task. The lesson Legal AI tools covers them.

Large, medium and small models

Most companies offer models in different sizes. Large models are the smartest and slowest, and they are best for long documents and hard problems. Small models are fast and cheap, and they are fine for quick rewrites. In Anthropic's Claude family, Haiku is small, Sonnet is medium and Opus is large. Words like "mini," "flash" or "lite" in a name usually mean a smaller, faster model.

Thinking models

Some models take extra time to work through a problem step by step before they answer. Some apps call this a "thinking" or "reasoning" setting. These models are slower and much better at multi-step problems, such as working out a deadline or comparing two contract versions.

Other ways tools differ

  • How much they can read at once. This is called the context window. A bigger one can handle a long contract or a deposition transcript.
  • How they treat your data. Business and enterprise plans often promise not to train on your data. Free consumer plans may not. This matters for client confidentiality.
  • Open models. Some models, such as Meta's Llama, can run on your organization's own computers, so data never leaves them.

Which tool to use for client work

For work involving clients, the right tool is usually the one your employer has approved and set up. The AI tools page lists common general and legal tools.
