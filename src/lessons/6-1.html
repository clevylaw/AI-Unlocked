More than chat

A chatbot answers you. An AI agent takes action. You give it a goal. It breaks the goal into steps and uses tools, such as a web browser, a document system or email. It checks its work and keeps going until the task is done.

Legal examples

"Review these 200 supplier contracts, find every clause about ending the contract early, and put them in a table with the notice period." Or: "Research how courts in this state have treated electronic signatures on wills, and draft a short memo with sources." An agent can attempt each of these with little help.

Agents you can use today

  • Research agents. The "deep research" modes in ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude read dozens of sources and write a report with links. Legal research services are adding similar features.
  • Agents that use a computer. Some assistants can operate a web browser. They click, type and fill in forms.
  • Document agents. Some legal tools can work through a whole set of documents and fill in a review table.

Stay in charge

An agent that can send emails, file documents or move money needs firm limits. Your duty to supervise covers the work it does, in the same way it covers work by a junior lawyer or a vendor.

  • Give it access only to what the task needs.
  • Require a person to approve anything that is sent, filed or paid.
  • Check its sources and results before you rely on them.
