Guessing the next word

A language model does one main job. It looks at some text and guesses which word comes next. Then it adds that word and guesses again. In training, it practices this billions of times on a huge amount of writing. After all that practice, it gets good at writing useful text.

Think of it this way

Your phone's keyboard suggests the next word as you type. A large language model does the same thing on a much bigger scale. It learned from a huge amount of writing, and it keeps the whole conversation in mind as it writes.

What "large" means

A model stores what it learned as billions of numbers that training adjusts. These numbers are called parameters. The biggest models have hundreds of billions of them or more. Companies rarely say the exact number. More parameters usually means more knowledge and skill. It also costs more to run.

What they learn from

Language models learn from books, websites, encyclopedias, computer code, science papers and online discussion boards. That is why chatbots know a little about almost everything.

How much they understand

Researchers still argue about how much these models understand. For you, one point matters most. Chatbots write text that sounds right, based on patterns. Usually that text is helpful, and sometimes it is wrong. So your own judgment still matters.
