Bar guidance on AI

Ethics opinions and guidance on lawyers’ use of generative AI from the ABA and several state bars, with links to the originals.

· Report a correction (opens GitHub in a new tab)

AI for Legal Work · https://clevylaw.github.io/AI-Unlocked/resources/bar-guidance/ · Content last reviewed November 2026

This list is a starting point, not a complete survey. Many other states have issued guidance, and opinions are updated. Check your own bar’s website for the current version.

American Bar Association
Formal Opinion 512, Generative Artificial Intelligence Tools (July 29, 2024). The national baseline. Covers competence, confidentiality, communication, candor, supervision and fees. Read the opinion (opens in a new tab)
California
State Bar of California practical guidance on generative AI, first approved in November 2023 and updated in 2026. Ethics and Technology Resources (opens in a new tab)
District of Columbia
D.C. Bar Ethics Opinion 388, Attorneys’ Use of Generative Artificial Intelligence in Client Matters (2024). Stresses that AI output does not replace checking facts and citations. Read the opinion (opens in a new tab)
Florida
Florida Bar Ethics Opinion 24-1 (January 19, 2024). Recommends informed client consent before disclosing confidential information to a third-party AI tool, and cautions against charging for time spent learning AI. Read the opinion (opens in a new tab)
Kentucky
Kentucky Bar Association Ethics Opinion KBA E-457 (2024). Listed on the KBA’s ethics opinions page under 2024. KBA ethics opinions (opens in a new tab)
North Carolina
2024 Formal Ethics Opinion 1, Use of Artificial Intelligence in a Law Practice (adopted November 1, 2024). Lawyers may use AI if they do so competently, protect confidentiality and supervise the work. Read the opinion (opens in a new tab)
Texas
Professional Ethics Committee Opinion 705 (February 2025). Among other points, lawyers should not bill hourly for time saved by using AI. Read the opinion (opens in a new tab)

Other places to check

Bars and courts in other states, including New York, New Jersey and Pennsylvania, have also issued reports, guidelines or opinions on AI. Search your state bar’s ethics opinions and your courts’ websites for the current versions. For court filings, also check local rules and each judge’s standing orders.
