Sample AI policy for a legal team

A short starting point for a law firm or legal department policy on generative AI. Adapt it to your own rules.

· Report a correction (opens GitHub in a new tab)

AI for Legal Work · https://clevylaw.github.io/AI-Unlocked/resources/sample-ai-policy/ · Content last reviewed November 2026

Read this first

This is a sample to adapt, not legal advice. Ethics rules and court rules differ by state and court. Review it with whoever handles ethics and risk for your organization. Replace everything in [brackets].

1. Purpose

This policy explains how people at [organization] may use generative AI tools in their work. It applies to lawyers, paralegals, staff, contractors and students working with us.

2. Approved tools

Use only the tools listed in [location of the approved list] for work that involves client or matter information. [Name or team] approves tools after reviewing how they store, use and protect data. Public chatbots on personal accounts may be used only with made-up or public information.

3. Client information

Do not enter confidential client information into any tool that is not approved for it. Before entering client information into a tool that may keep, learn from or share it, get the client’s informed consent where the rules require it. Remove names and identifying details when the task does not need them.

4. Checking AI output

The person who uses AI is responsible for the result. Check every fact, date, number, quote and legal authority against a trusted source before relying on it or sending it. Find and read every case, statute and rule cited, and confirm it is still good law.

5. Court filings

Before filing, check the court’s local rules and the judge’s standing orders on AI and follow them, including any disclosure. A lawyer who signs a filing must have personally confirmed every authority in it.

6. Supervision and training

Supervising lawyers must make sure the people they supervise follow this policy. Everyone who uses approved tools completes [training] before first use and when the tools or this policy change.

7. Billing

Bill only for time actually spent. Do not bill clients for time spent learning a general AI tool. Explain any charges for AI tools to clients in advance, in writing.

8. Records

Keep a short note in the matter file when AI was used for substantive work, including the tool and how the output was checked.

9. Problems and questions

Report any mistake, data exposure or concern to [contact] right away. Ask [contact] if you are unsure whether a use is allowed.

10. Review

[Name or team] reviews this policy at least every [six] months and when the law or ethics guidance changes. Last reviewed: [date].
