AI use checklist

A one-page check to run before, during and after you use AI on legal work.

· Report a correction (opens GitHub in a new tab)

AI for Legal Work · https://clevylaw.github.io/AI-Unlocked/resources/ai-use-checklist/ · Content last reviewed November 2026

Based on the duties described in ABA Formal Opinion 512 and state bar guidance. Your own state’s rules and your employer’s policy come first.

Before you start

  • The tool is approved by my employer or school for this kind of work.
  • I know whether the tool keeps, trains on or shares what I type.
  • I will not enter client names, privileged material or other confidential information unless the tool is approved for it.
  • If the rules require it, the client has given informed consent to using this tool with their information.
  • I have checked the court’s local rules and the judge’s standing orders on AI, if this is for a filing.
  • For coursework, I have checked my school’s and professor’s AI rules.

While you work

  • I use made-up names and only the facts the task needs.
  • My prompt names my role, the jurisdiction, the task and the format I want.
  • I ask the tool to flag what it is unsure about.
  • I keep a short note of what I used AI for on this matter.

Before it leaves your desk

  • I found and read every case, statute and rule cited, from a trusted source.
  • Each authority is still good law, checked with a citator.
  • Every quote, page number, date and dollar amount matches the original.
  • The facts are right for this client and this matter.
  • I rewrote the draft in my own words and I stand behind every sentence.
  • I made any AI disclosure the court or client requires.
  • My bill reflects time I actually spent, and any AI costs have been explained to the client.

If you supervise others

  • The people I supervise know our AI policy and which tools are approved.
  • I ask how they checked any AI-assisted work before I rely on it or sign it.
